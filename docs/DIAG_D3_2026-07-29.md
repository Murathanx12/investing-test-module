> 
> **R2.** The deciding metric is a paired Diebold-Mariano test on the per-state Brier DIFFERENCE series (engine − persistence), per outcome. Variance via BOTH Newey-West (lag = horizon in months) AND a circular block bootstrap (block length 12, ≥2000 resamples) — report both. BSS is reported as DESCRIPTIVE ONLY and must be labeled so.
> 
> **R3.** Report the Brier decomposition REL − RES + UNC (10 probability bins) separately for engine and persistence, per outcome. RESOLUTION is the payable part — say which forecaster has more of it.
> 
> **R4.** Report N_eff ≈ n(1−r₁)/(1+r₁) (Santer et al. 2000) computed on each Brier-difference series, alongside raw n.

## 3. R3 — Brier decomposition REL − RES + UNC (10 bins)

RESOLUTION (RES) is the payable part: it is the only term that says the forecaster separates high-outcome from low-outcome states. Reliability (REL) is fixable by recalibration; uncertainty (UNC) belongs to the outcome, not the forecaster.

| crash12m_dd15 | 0.420 | 0.1371 | 0.0402 | 0.2086 | 0.0010 | 0.2437 | **engine** |
| crash12m_dd20 | 0.181 | 0.0565 | 0.0191 | 0.1524 | 0.0000 | 0.1485 | **engine** |

### Where the Brier win actually comes from (UNC cancels in the difference)

Brier(eng) − Brier(pers) = (REL_eng − REL_pers) − (RES_eng − RES_pers). Reliability is a hedging term: persistence is forced to forecast 0 or 1, so it is maximally unreliable by construction, and any hedged probability collects that gap **without forecasting anything**. Only ΔRES is payable.

| outcome | Brier gap | from reliability (ΔREL) | from resolution (ΔRES) | share of gap from hedging | engine RES / UNC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| fwd6m_positive | 0.1316 | 0.1166 | 0.0165 | 87.6% | 10.0% |
| fwd12m_positive | 0.0721 | 0.0360 | 0.0400 | 47.3% | 29.3% |
| crash12m_dd15 | 0.1084 | 0.0715 | 0.0392 | 64.6% | 16.5% |
| crash12m_dd20 | 0.1109 | 0.0959 | 0.0190 | 83.4% | 12.8% |

**Read this before reading the verdicts.** Resolution is the fraction of the outcome's own uncertainty the forecaster actually resolves; the last column is that fraction (10.0 / 29.3 / 16.5 / 12.8% here — highest on `fwd12m_positive`). And because REL_eng > RES_eng on **all four** outcomes, the engine's miscalibration exceeds its resolution everywhere: a **constant forecast at the base rate would score a strictly better Brier than the engine** (by +0.031 / +0.044 / +0.095 / +0.040). The verdicts in §2 are the pre-registered decision rule and are reported unaltered, but a Brier win built almost entirely from ΔREL against a 0/1 baseline is a win for *hedging*, not for *information*. *(Corrected 2026-07-29 post-verification: the original paragraph here was copied from the 63-td run's numbers and understated this finding in the engine's favour.)*

## 8. Caveats that limit what any of the above can mean

1. **Outcome-aggregation leak closed at this exclusion.** With 504 td of self-exclusion, every analog's 6m/12m/24m forward window ends at or before the query date (the boundary analog's 24m window ends exactly at it). Standardization and outcome aggregation are both causal in this run. (The 63-td companion report `DIAG_D3_2026-07-29_exc63.md` did carry this leak — which is why crash12m_dd15/dd20 downgraded from BEATS to INCONCLUSIVE here.)
2. **Overlapping monthly states.** Consecutive states share most of their forward window; N_eff above is the honest count.
3. **One market, one history.** All four outcomes are functions of the same SPY path; the four tests are not independent.
4. **The baseline is binary, the engine is probabilistic.** Persistence as specified emits 0/1, so its Brier score is its error RATE and it is maximally penalized for being wrong. A hedged probabilistic forecaster wins that comparison on reliability alone. This asymmetry is baked into the pre-registered rule (R1) and the verdicts respect it, but §3's ΔRES column — not the DM t — is what says whether the engine knows anything.
5. Causal standardization costs the first ~2 years of the library (43 states unscoreable, first scoreable 2006-09-29).

Findings that qualify the read, none altering a computed verdict:

1. **Degenerate-analog states, undisclosed in the first draft.** 59 of the 239

   block — were built on < 50 accepted analogs (min 3, 9 states < 10):
   `retrieve_analogs` guards the *candidate* count, not the *acceptance* count
   under NMS. Acceptance counts are recorded in each state's
   `extras.n_analogs_used`. Sensitivity (period selection, NOT a corrected
   number): dropping states with < 25 analogs strengthens every t

4. **fwd6m BEATS is block-length-fragile.** Robust to NW lag (−2.08…−2.51,

   block lengths 18 and 24. Block 12 was pre-registered, so the verdict
