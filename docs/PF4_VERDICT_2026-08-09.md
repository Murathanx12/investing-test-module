**A second 0 %-assignment path, found by the cross-check and previously
undisclosed.** The delisting audit counted 272 forced-liquidation events from
the book's own holdings while the engine reports 602. They are different sets,
and the difference is the finding: the engine's dead-name check runs *before*
each rebalance, so a name that is **freshly selected into the book in a month
where its return is missing** never reaches that check and is instead handed a
silent `fillna(0.0)` in the realization step — no liquidation, no cost. 272
name-months out of ~72,300 (0.38 %).

Empirically benign here, and the reason is checkable rather than asserted: of
those 272, **zero** were performance-coded delists with a missing CRSP `DLRET`.
They are mergers and exchanges, which pay out at roughly the last price, so 0 %
is approximately correct for the stub. Disclosed because the *next* strategy
that holds a different population may not be so lucky, and because a silent
fallback that happens to be harmless is still a silent fallback.

**Three things about costs.** The mechanical tick floor is a *lower bound* —
median 54.3 bps pre-1997 against the 25 bps the spec assumed, 26.0 bps
1997-2001, 3.3 bps after decimalization. The spine carries no daily high/low, so
Corwin-Schultz and Abdi-Ranaldo are not computable here, and any excess left
standing is an upper bound. And the pre-2001 collapse is a **turnover** problem,
which §4 resolves.

## 5. The controls

### 5a. The characteristic-matched placebo — the reviewer's kill-shot, and it fails to kill

100 random books, each name replaced by a partner drawn from the **same
dollar-volume × momentum × book-to-market decile cell**, held for exactly as
long as the real name is held. Only the profitability dimension is randomized.
Cell fallback rate 0.6 %; 77.8 % of names matched on all three characteristics.

| basis | strategy | placebo mean | placebo p95 | placebo max | empirical p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gross | **+6.05 %** | +2.92 % | +3.69 % | +4.05 % | **0.0099** |
| net | **+4.67 %** | +1.28 % | +2.03 % | +2.40 % | **0.0099** |

**The strategy beats all 100 draws on both bases.** Note the placebo mean is
*higher* than a naive random book's would be — matching on size, liquidity and
momentum hands the control those premia by construction, which is exactly why
this is the harder gate. It is now the edge gate; turnover matching is demoted
to a construction check.

Recorded against the house: **H-4 predicted this placebo would be harder
(p between 0.0099 and 0.05). It was not — it returned the same floor value,
because the strategy beat every draw. MISS.**

Recorded caveat that cuts the other way: the control's realized turnover ran
2.93 against the strategy's 2.40, because a substitute that loses eligibility
must be replaced. Charging the control more cost than the strategy would flatter
the strategy — the exact error class found in the NIGHT-3 placebo — which is why
the **gross** band is the primary statistic and the net band is printed beside
it. Both give the same answer.

### 5b. The memory ladder — our own control was a high draw

`DIAG-PF4-MEMORY-PLACEBO-2`: the situations-only arm NIGHT-3 never ran, plus
13 within-regime shuffled seeds instead of one global-permutation seed.

| arm | standalone excess |
|---|---|
| A — no memory | +4.67 % |
| **situations-only** (block shown, outcomes withheld) | **+3.74 %** |
| shuffled, mean of 13 seeds | +3.60 % (sd 1.20 %, range 1.63-5.88 %) |
| E — real memory | +6.21 % |

* NIGHT-3's single seed printed **+5.07 %**, which sits at roughly the **92nd
  percentile** of the 13-seed distribution. A high draw, not an extreme one —
  and with six seeds it looked like an outlier above all of them, which is
  itself a lesson about reading six draws.
* Corrected paired statistic: **E − pooled shuffled = +2.45 %/yr at NW t 1.48**
  against a 2.96 %/yr MDE — against the +1.13 % at t 0.43 NIGHT-3 published.
  Roughly **twice the effect**, still short of t ≥ 2.
* **Situations-only lands BELOW no-memory.** Telling the model it has seen a
  situation before while withholding what happened is worse than not mentioning
  memory at all.

> **Verdict: the memory-content question is REOPENED, not answered.** NIGHT-3's
> text ("content not demonstrated") survives as a statement — it is still not
> demonstrated at t ≥ 2 — but its *basis* was defective and the effect is about
> double what we reported. A registered successor is warranted.

Methodological point recorded because it nearly went the other way: E's z-score
inside the seed distribution is 2.18, and it was 3.67 when only six seeds had
landed. That z is against **permutation** noise — every seed shares the same
months and the same slates — and is far too tight to support an out-of-sample
claim. The month-level paired difference is the estimator that answers the
question, and the aggregator now says so instead of quoting the z.

### 5c. Marginal-decile alphas — R-5, tested properly

The fifteen ten-name windows inside ranks 1-150 have alphas ranging from −1.68 %
to +8.64 %, which *looks* like structure. It is not: each window is a ten-name
book with a median standard error of 2.92 %, and a Cochran's Q homogeneity test
gives **Q = 12.38 on 14 df, p = 0.58**. **Flat is not rejected. R-5 HIT.**

And the complementary result, which is *not* range-restricted and is therefore
the cleanest membership evidence in the programme
(`DIAG-PF4-BOUNDARY-1`):

| band | mean ten-name alpha | windows positive |
|---|---|---|
| ranks 1-150 | **+3.84 %/yr** | 14 of 15 |
| ranks 151-300 | **−0.85 %/yr** | 6 of 15 |

A **4.7 percentage-point gap across the membership cut**, with flatness inside
it. Membership carries real information; ordering inside the selected set does
not measurably. That is the synthesis NIGHT-3 reached by an invalid route and
this campaign reached by a valid one.
